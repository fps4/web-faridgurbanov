## What this is

Two years into AI, most organizations look the same. Nearly everyone has a personal copilot, one team
works on something ambitious, and no use case runs across the organization, built or bought.

This storyline is the route from there to one AI product that works. Which decision to fund. How to
test it before you buy. What it takes to run, how people will use it, what can go wrong, and how to get
it approved. It follows the order the work happens in, and the field's own frameworks, each named in
the part that uses it.

Every part ends in your organization: the move, who makes it, how you will know it worked, and a
download for next week's meeting.

## Who it is for

You do not need a technical background. Each part says whose move it is.

- **Heads of department and sponsors.** You hold a budget and want one use case live. Parts 1, 6 and 7
  are yours: which decision to fund, the risks, and the proposal that gets a yes.
- **Product owners and product managers.** You turn the idea into something a team can build and
  test: the approach, the scorecard and the design for the people who use it, in Parts 2, 3 and 5.
- **Delivery, operations and IT leads.** You make it run and keep it running: the system around the
  model, the gates before go-live, and in Part 8, building it with coding agents.

## What you get in every part

- **A page.** The step, the idea behind it with its source, and how it plays out on the example. About
  ten minutes to read.
- **Two short episodes.** About five minutes each: a diagram that draws itself while I talk it
  through.
- **Downloads.** A canvas, a scorecard, a register or a plan. Blank for your case, filled in for the
  example, and free to adapt (CC BY 4.0).
- **On Monday.** One thing to do in your own organization, and how you will know it worked.

## One example, all eight parts

A mid-size insurer, where a claims handler reads every small home-contents claim by hand. The head of
claims wants the clear ones paid the same day. Every part takes the next step on the same claims, the
same numbers and the same people, so the eight parts read as one product being decided and built.

- **68,000** small property claims a year, up to €2,500 each.
- **40 minutes** at the desk for every one of them.
- **8 days** before the customer is paid.
- **€2.5 million** a year to handle them.

The insurer is fictional and its numbers are synthetic. The facts about the insurance market around it
are real, with their sources. Each part also shows the same step in a bank or a retailer.

## Questions

### Is this a course?

No. There is no test and no certificate. It is a playbook: each part gives you a template to use on
your own case, in your own meetings.

### Do I need to be technical?

No. The parts are written for the people who fund, own and run an AI product. Where a term matters,
the page explains it once. Part 8 is the most technical, and says so.

### We are not an insurer. Is it still useful?

Yes. The insurer is one worked example. The steps are the same for any decision where waiting for
information is the cost, and every part shows the same step in another sector.

### What does it cost?

Nothing. The pages and episodes are free, and the downloads are CC BY 4.0: use them, adapt them and
share them, with credit.

### Is there a Dutch version?

English first. The episodes get Dutch subtitles, and Dutch pages follow later.
