I just find engineering very much fun. I get fascinated while building things that work, and that can solve problems.

My problem with pure architecture work is that I can't feel it the way I feel when I'm behind the steering wheel. It is the difference between driving an F1 car, and watching and guiding it.

I had a time when I designed and others implemented. I made wrong time commitments, which resulted in longer delivery timelines and in frustration, first of all for myself.

Now with AI things are changing. I feel quite a potential in having a tool that executes on your vision. You turn a high level concept into actionable steps, and then you get it step by step closer to what you expect it to be. It is a really exciting journey, and I hear the excitement of fellow engineers as well. Some are scared to lose their jobs, but I am not. As long as you bring in valuable ideas and guide the system on their implementation, it is totally a tool and not a replacement for humans.

The work setup is changing too. I expect the DevOps rituals and the concept of a sprint to change as well. One example: in my own project I have a one-week sprint, instead of the two weeks that I know from client work. I also define the sprint budget not as the size of a team of humans, but as a budget for the LLM and for my time: the time I can spend on setting the task, reviewing and confirming the execution.

On my own projects I am becoming a technical product owner of an agentic delivery team. I am building my own tool, maestro, to support this development process.
